# Writing

The voice of an engineering manager thinking out loud: honest, curious, humble without false modesty.

## Voice

- **First person.** "I've been testing", not "It has been observed".
- **Plain English.** Short sentences. One idea per sentence, one idea per slide.
- **No hype.** If a sentence would fit any AI product launch, rewrite it. Starting list of words to avoid: *revolutionary, game-changer, unlock, supercharge, seamless, leverage* (as a verb), *delve, robust* (unless measured).
- **Calibrated claims.** Prefer "can" to "might", and both to absolutes. Show evidence when you have it.
- **Invite challenge.** "Some might be wrong, and I'd like them challenged." "What are you seeing?"
- **Trust the reader.** Don't explain the metaphor or the joke.

## Mechanics

- **American spelling** ("color", "organize").
- **No em dashes.** Use a colon, comma, parentheses or a full stop.
- **Full stops, not exclamation marks**, on slides and in headlines. Long-form text can be warmer.
- **Sentence case** for headlines and new headings (proposed). Named things keep their Title Case, such as the four pillars and the principle names.
- Emoji: fine in a personal intro or a post, never in headlines, slides or docs.

## Story

- **Clear arc.** Hook, tension, turn, close.
- **Kickers as story beats.** Short italic lead-ins ending with a colon: "Before AI, every team asked:" / "Now:" / "The catch:".
- **Metaphor only at the edges.** The cooking metaphor opens and closes; it does not run through the middle.
- **Small playful touches** are welcome when they close a loop ("First case study is in the oven. More soon.").

## For AI-assisted drafts

- Disclose AI help where it matters, as in [the principles](https://github.com/miguelmog10/ai-assisted-software-engineering#authors-note): the ideas and decisions are mine; AI helped structure and polish.
- Read the draft aloud. If it does not sound like me talking to a peer, it is not done.
